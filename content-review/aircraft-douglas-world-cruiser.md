| Ordered by the Army Air Service in 1923; five built; a modified DT-2 torpedo bomber with fuel raised from 115 to 644 gallons, a different vertical tail, cockpits moved closer together, two radiator sizes, wheels or floats; 420-hp Liberty V-12; prototype built in 45 days for $23,721 and delivered November 1923; March 17, 1924 departure from Clover Field for Seattle; Boeing employees swapped wheels for floats in Seattle; April 6 departure from Sand Point, Lake Washington; Seattle crashed April 30, crew walked 10 days to Dutch Harbor; floats replaced with wheels at Calcutta June 26; Boston forced landing August 3; Boston II (the prototype) joined in Newfoundland; return September 28; 27,553 miles, 371 flying hours, 28 countries; motto "First Around the World"; specs: first flight November 1923, wingspan 50 ft, speed 103 mph, range 2,200 miles, crew of two | Boeing, *Select Products in Boeing History* (Douglas World Cruiser entry), https://www.boeing.com/content/dam/boeing/v2/company/history/pdf/Boeing_Products.pdf | ✅ |
| Six legs; May 15 crossing to the Komandorski Islands called "the first flight across the Pacific Ocean"; 74 pre-arranged landing areas; each plane carried only 300 pounds of supplies; earth inductor compass and no radio; Seattle crashed near Point Moeller, Alaska; Boston forced down on August 3 flying to Iceland, crew rescued; 27,550 miles; total flight time 371 hours 11 minutes; Lowell Smith's "Not in a million years" reply | NASM, "The Story of the First Flight Around the World", https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/story-first-flight-around-world | ✅ |
