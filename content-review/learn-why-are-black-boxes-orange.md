| Claim | Source | Status |
|---|---|---|
| EASA's 2018 review noted that most flight recorder models are already bright orange and proposed to retain only that color (the existing text allows bright orange or bright yellow, with reflective tape to help location under water) | EASA NPA 2018-03, *Flight recorders*, CS-25 and CS-29 text and rationale ("most models of flight recorders are already bright orange, it is proposed to only retain this colour"), https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/downloads/46032/en | ✅ |
| The NTSB puts "black boxes" in quotation marks; the origin of the nickname is not settled by official sources | NTSB, "Cockpit Voice Recorders and Flight Data Recorders", https://www.ntsb.gov/news/Pages/cvr_fdr.aspx (uses the term in quotation marks; gives no origin) | ✅ The page makes no claim about the origin |
| FDR records time, altitude, airspeed, heading, attitude, and items such as flap position, autopilot mode and smoke alarms; newly manufactured aircraft must record at least 88 parameters; some units record more than 1,000; older units used quarter-inch magnetic tape; FDR duration 25 hours | NTSB CVR/FDR page (text and specification table) | ✅ |
| CVR: microphone usually on the overhead panel between the pilots; captures crew voices, engine noise, stall warnings, gear movement, ATC communications, automated weather broadcasts and crew intercom; four channels; 30 minutes (older) or 2 hours (solid-state) | NTSB CVR/FDR page | ✅ |
| Crash survivability: 3,400 g for 6.5 ms; 1,100°C for 30 minutes; submerged to 20,000 feet | NTSB CVR/FDR page specification table | ✅ |
| Underwater locator beacon: activates when immersed; 37.5 kHz; needs a special receiver; detectable from depths down to 14,000 feet; battery shelf life of 6 years or more and 30-day operation once activated | NTSB CVR/FDR page | ✅ |
| CID test, December 1, 1984: remotely piloted Boeing 720, no one aboard, Rogers Dry Lake, Edwards AFB; crash-test dummies; 97% of sensors working at impact; antimisting fuel setback; FDR experiments largely successful | App lesson `black-boxes` and its Hangar card (NASA NTRS 19860012462; NASA TM-4084) | ✅ App facts |
| David Warren, a chemist specializing in aviation fuels at the Aeronautical Research Laboratories, Melbourne; the Comet losses and the Minifon miniature tape recorder prompted the idea; team Kenneth Fraser, Lane Sear and Walter Boswell; demonstration model 1957; not taken up by Australian authorities, received enthusiastically in Britain; after the 1960 Mackay Fokker Friendship crash the court of inquiry judge ordered recorders on future Australian aircraft; Australia first to make cockpit voice recording mandatory | Defence Science and Technology Group (Australian Department of Defence), "Black box flight recorder", https://www.dst.defence.gov.au/innovation/black-box-flight-recorder | ⚠️ dst.defence.gov.au is an official government laboratory page but not in the brief's allow-list (the ATSB and National Museum of Australia pages on the same story could not be opened from this environment). The invention year varies between accounts: DST gives 1953 for the idea; the National Museum of Australia's search summary dates Warren's memo to March 19, 1954; a DST obituary gives 1956 for the first prototype. The page therefore says "the early 1950s", attributes 1953 to the defence agency, and gives 1957 for the demonstration model, which the accounts agree on. |
| The Comet entered service May 2, 1952 as the first jet airliner and suffered losses in 1954 | App lesson `comet-metal-fatigue`; FAA Lessons Learned, De Havilland DH-106 Comet 1, https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/G-ALYV | ✅ |
| NTSB 2018 report: the 2-hour standard; a 30-minute CVR was overwritten by the remaining 2 hours 11 minutes of a 4-hour flight; some international flights last over 12 hours; ICAO 25-hour standard for aircraft over 27,000 kg manufactured after January 1, 2021; recommendations A-18-30 (new aircraft) and A-18-31 (retrofit) | NTSB ASR-18-04, *Extended Duration Cockpit Voice Recorders* (October 2018), https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/AccidentReports/Reports/ASR1804.pdf | ✅ |
| FAA proposed the 25-hour rule in December 2023 (NPRM published December 4, 2023) | FAA NPRM, Notice No. 24-04, https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/media/25-Hr-CVR_NPRM_Issuance.pdf; Federal Register final rule background | ✅ The app left the year out; the Federal Register's final rule states the NPRM publication date as December 4, 2023 |
| FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 signed May 16, 2024 (Public Law 118-63), section 366 requires 25-hour CVRs; final rule issued February 2, 2026 (2026-02110, 91 FR 4447); applies to aircraft manufactured on or after May 16, 2025 used in Part 121 service or transport-category aircraft with 30 or more seats; other aircraft already required to carry a CVR: manufactured on or after February 2, 2027 (27,000 kg or more, 29 or fewer seats) and February 2, 2029 (26,999 kg or less); existing Part 121 and 30-plus-seat aircraft by May 16, 2030 | FAA InFO 26013 (August 10, 2026), https://www.faa.gov/other_visit/aviation_industry/airline_operators/airline_safety/info/all_infos/InFO26013.pdf | ✅ Dated on the page as "as of October 2026" |
